SecOps · Security

See the risks that still need a person.

Scanners already find problems. SecBoard lines them up in one list so you can see what is still open, how serious it is, who owns it, and how long it has been waiting.

What is still open

  • Posture score
  • Open critical and open high
  • Items overdue past SLA
  • Known exploited CVEs
  • Open versus closed and closure rate
  • Compliance status across configured frameworks

How a fix moves along

  • Severity distribution and score bands
  • Status: open, in progress, resolved, accepted
  • Resolution type, including fixed, exception, and false positive
  • Aging buckets and mean time to remediate
  • Lifecycle funnel from detection to accepted risk
  • Assignee on each vulnerability

Cloud checks

  • Check results and run history
  • Severity, pass or fail, service, region, and resource
  • Coverage warnings when permissions skip checks
  • Asset exposure and high-risk targets
  • Alerts, activity, and reports
  • Tool findings kept next to the normalized queue

FinOps · Cost

See the bill before you try to shrink it.

First, know where the money goes and who owns it. Then look at waste. SecBoard keeps the history so a saving is something you can explain, not a surprise cut.

  • What you have spent this month, and what you spent last month.
  • A forecast of the monthly bill, and an estimate of waste.
  • Resources that are running, and resources nobody owns.
  • Daily spend for the last 30 days, split by the services that cost the most.
  • Spend by team or app, when those tags exist.
  • Problems called out as idle, wasted, orphaned, or untracked.

How the cost score works

A higher score means more of the bill has an owner, and less of it is waste. Open cost problems pull the score down until someone deals with them.

FAQ

Straight answers.

What is SecBoard?

It is one place to see if your systems are safe, fast, and worth what you pay. Maatara Consulting built it so security, performance, cost, and a plain-language assistant share the same list of systems.

What do PerfOps and AIOps do?

PerfOps shows whether things are fast and stable: what is slow, what is failing, and which service is under strain. AIOps lets you ask what to look at next. The answer points back to the evidence, and a person still decides.

Does SecBoard replace our security tools?

No. An agent runs the tools you already have and sends the results back. Your scanners and cloud accounts stay in place.

How does the cost score work?

It mixes four things: how much spend is allocated (30%), how much has an owner (25%), how little is wasted (30%), and how many cost problems are still open (15%).

How do we know which risk to fix first?

You can see how serious it is, whether attackers are already using it, how old it is, which system it sits on, and who it is assigned to. The score is a guide. A person still decides.

Can we trust an AI answer on its own?

No. The assistant can miss something or get it wrong. Use it to get to the evidence faster, then have a person check anything that changes a system or the bill.

Does SecBoard prove we are compliant or stop a breach?

No. It can show how you are doing against the checks you turn on. It does not issue a compliance certificate, and it does not promise a breach will not happen.
